pageFaqs:
  - question: "Can a 'pennies on the dollar' tax resolution company really settle my debt for almost nothing?"
    answer:
      - "Rarely, and be skeptical of anyone advertising it as a certainty. Qualifying for an offer in compromise depends on specific financial criteria the IRS applies to every taxpayer, and many people who pay for these services never actually qualify."
  - question: "What's the difference between an offer in compromise and an installment agreement?"
    answer:
      - "An offer in compromise settles the debt for less than what's owed, in a lump sum or short-term payments, and requires meeting strict eligibility criteria."
      - "An installment agreement instead spreads the full amount owed over time. Which one fits depends on your income, assets, and the size of the liability."
  - question: "What is Currently Not Collectible status?"
    answer:
      - "It's a status the IRS grants when a taxpayer genuinely cannot pay anything toward their tax debt without covering basic living expenses. It pauses active collection, though the debt and any applicable interest generally remain."
  - question: "I think the IRS says I owe more than I actually do. What can I do?"
    answer:
      - "There are procedures to challenge an incorrect assessment, separate from debt-reduction programs like an offer in compromise. Getting the underlying number right often matters more than negotiating around a wrong one."

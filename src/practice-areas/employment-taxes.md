pageFaqs:
  - question: "Can I really be held personally liable for my business's unpaid payroll taxes?"
    answer:
      - "Yes. Owners, officers, and other 'responsible persons' who willfully fail to withhold and remit employment taxes can face the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty under IRC § 6672 — a personal assessment separate from the business's own liability."
  - question: "Should I still file employment tax returns if I can't pay what's owed?"
    answer:
      - "Yes. Filing and paying are separate legal duties, and each carries its own IRS penalty. Not filing because you can't pay only adds a second penalty on top of the first, and can accelerate IRS enforcement."
  - question: "Can unpaid employment taxes lead to criminal charges?"
    answer:
      - "They can, and the IRS has increased criminal prosecutions for unpaid employment taxes in recent years. The government's goal in these cases is typically to hold the responsible individual accountable, not just collect the money."
  - question: "What happens once a Revenue Officer opens a case against my business?"
    answer:
      - "A Revenue Officer will investigate the failure to deposit and begin building a case for personal liability under the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty against responsible individuals, in addition to pursuing the business itself. Early representation matters — by the time the IRS formally notifies an individual of potential liability, the case is often already well developed."
